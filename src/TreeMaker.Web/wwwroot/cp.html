Fold a crease pattern

Open a crease pattern, check its lines, and see it folded flat. Vector files (.svg, .fold) are read exactly. Images (.png, .jpg) are traced. Red lines are mountains, blue lines valleys and yellow lines flat; dark lines are the border in a coloured pattern, and otherwise mountains when solid and valleys when dashed (the border along the outline). Check the traced lines before folding. This page is separate from the session and keeps nothing.

1. Crease pattern

How the lines are read

SVG: every line, polyline, polygon, rect and straight path. Red strokes are mountains, blue valleys, yellow or grey flat, green a cut (the border) and magenta a crease with no fold given. Where the folds are coloured, a solid black stroke is the border, wherever it lies. Otherwise a stroke with no colour of its own is a mountain when solid and a valley when dashed (stroke-dasharray), unless that option is off, when it is a crease with no mountain or valley given; on the outline it is the border. A title containing "mountain" or "valley" also sets it. Ends that nearly meet are joined. FOLD: edges_assignment M, V, B, F, U and C (a cut); a folded form is refused.

Images: the pixels are sorted by colour, straight lines are found in each colour (a Hough transform) and fitted to their pixels, and their ends are joined and snapped. Along each dark line, the share covered by ink tells a dashed line from a solid one: gaps between dashes are bridged, so a dashed line is read as one line. Swap mountains and valleys exchanges every assignment, which folds the same shape from the paper's other side. Click a line in the drawing to change it: mountain → valley → unassigned → flat → removed.

3. Folded flat

    The folded faces are drawn see-through, the paper's front in one colour and its back in another. Layer order is not worked out. Red rings on the lines mark vertices that fail Kawasaki's or Maekawa's theorem.
